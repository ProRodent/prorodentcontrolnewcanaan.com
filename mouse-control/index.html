How mice live inside a New Canaan house.

Few rodents settle indoors as completely as the house mouse. It nests in wall cavities, under appliances, in boxes of holiday ornaments and in attic insulation, and food is never far away. One pair can multiply into a crowd within months.

The clues are small. Droppings resemble dark grains of rice, wrappers and soft plastic show fine tooth marks, and scratching inside a wall may continue a while before it stops. Mice work mostly after dark, so many people first notice them when the house falls quiet.

New Canaan has a reputation for mid-century modern design, yet gaps appear in houses of every style. The building shapes the plan more than the street does, and ten minutes with a flashlight along the foundation and roof edge often shows the entry.

Following the route before any trap goes down.

The technician begins where sign is heaviest and works outward. New pellets, oily smears and nibbled corners trace a route, and every route ends at an entry. Trapping blind takes a few animals but leaves the cause open.

  • Peer under sinks, behind the stove and fridge, and inside the water heater closet.
  • Inspect garage door corners, the rubber bottom seal and the seam where wall meets slab.
  • Scan the attic hatch and the vent edges, and wherever plumbing or wiring enters the house.
  • Listen near a basement ceiling and a bathtub access panel for scratching.

Catch the animals, then shut the openings.

Snap traps and enclosed stations are set along the evidence, away from children and pets. Mice run the wall line and check out anything new, so a trap against the baseboard beats one in open floor.

When early catches show who is active, sealing can start. Steel wool by itself fails because mice drag it out. Each opening gets metal flashing, mesh and sealant, with materials listed on the exclusion page.

Small steps while you wait for the visit.

Keep the broom away from dry pellets and loose bait out of the house. Sweeping sends dust aloft. To clean up before the visit, follow the damp method in the CDC pages linked at the bottom.

Storage matters. Dry goods and pet food belong in sealed tubs, and bowls of food and water should come up overnight. This will not solve things by itself, but it removes the reward that keeps drawing mice back.

Once the traps have done their job.

A good outcome looks like no fresh pellets, quiet nights and empty traps. A badly soiled attic or wall space calls for attic and crawl space cleanup, and gnawed cables or insulation go through rodent damage repair, priced on its own.

Unsure whether mice or something bigger is behind it? Start with an inspection, which ends with the animal identified and a plan in hand.

Handling rodent waste with no dust in the air: what to do

Early caution lowers the odds of inhaling contaminated dust, and the steps are taken from the CDC cleanup page.

  • Hold the broom. Dry droppings crumble, and sweeping turns them into dust you could inhale.
  • If a space has been shut for weeks, throw it open and walk away for roughly thirty minutes.
  • Gloves go on before anything soiled is touched, and a careful handwash ends the task.
  • The order is soak, wait, wipe, with roughly five minutes of contact time in the middle.
  • Choose a sealed bag over an open bin for waste, and use gloves or a tool on a carcass.
  • Muscle aches, fever or breathlessness after exposure are medical questions and not pest questions.

Mouse control questions.

How small a hole will a mouse use?

Picture a pencil poked into drywall. A gap around a pipe, behind a dryer vent, at a door corner or where a cable enters a wall can be that wide.

Do mice move in when it turns cold?

Frequently. Frost and snow cover send small animals in search of warm, dry shelter, and a heated house fits. Some gap you ignored all summer may become their favorite door by November.

Can I set out store-bought poison meanwhile?

Store shelves offer households only bait stations that arrive loaded, and any mouse that eats from one may crawl into the wall and die. Check with the technician first.

When will the scratching stop?

It depends on how many mice there are and whether the entry points get closed. After the walk-through you receive a realistic timeline.

Do mouse calls get answered overnight?

Yes. A person picks up at every hour, and a technician may visit the same day if the schedule allows.
