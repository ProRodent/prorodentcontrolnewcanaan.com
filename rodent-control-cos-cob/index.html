Basements and crawl spaces.

A foundation wall meets soil along its whole length, and any crack, vent or pipe sleeve is a possible entry. Look for gaps around utility lines, loose crawl space screens and window wells with no cover.

Mice need only a pencil-width gap. If you notice scratching below the first floor or pellets near a furnace or water heater, say so on the call. Garage and basement control covers what a lower-level visit includes.

Before you call: handling droppings

Early caution lowers the odds of inhaling contaminated dust, and the steps are taken from the CDC cleanup page.

  • Never dry-sweep or dry-vacuum pellets, stains or nest scraps.
  • A garage, shed or attic closed up for a while needs air before you work in it, so open it and wait around half an hour.
  • Begin with hand protection. End with a thorough wash.
  • Saturate the pellets with disinfectant, let them sit five minutes, then take them up with towels.
  • Wipes and droppings go into a bag that closes tightly, and a carcass never touches bare skin.
  • Fever, aches or breathing difficulty after contact with waste deserve a physician's attention. We make no claims about anyone's health.
