From address to action plan.

Because Norwalk is a city with a mix of property types, we do not assume anything about your address. Tell us whether the house has an attached garage, a basement or a crawl space, and whether a car is usually parked outdoors. Those answers decide where the walk-through begins.

Garages and basements serve as quiet back doors. Seals flatten, screens tear and stored cartons supply nesting material. Few people inspect them, so trouble can go unseen for weeks.

Garages and parked cars.

An attached garage is a common route into a house, since the wall between garage and kitchen holds pipes and wires. The technician checks the door seal, the threshold and every pipe and wire passing into the living space.

A car parked outdoors or left for days can become a nest. Peek under the hood and at the cabin vents, and read the vehicle page. If you find nest material, hold off on starting the engine.

Where the calendar matters.

Deep winter keeps animals under cover, and a warm dry attic looks like a good home. If noises start after a cold snap, note the date and the room, which helps trace the way in.

Safety steps for waste and nests

Public health agencies give identical advice to every household. Nothing on this list is unique to a visit from us.

  • Disturbing dry droppings is what causes trouble, so keep brooms and shop vacuums out of the picture.
  • Ventilation is step one for a musty shed or crawl space: about thirty minutes of open doors.
  • Germs are invisible, so gloves on, then soap and water when you finish.
  • Disinfect before you collect. Wet the waste fully and leave it roughly five minutes.
  • Wipes and droppings go into a bag that closes tightly, and a carcass never touches bare skin.
  • Not well a few weeks later? A clinician is the one to ask, so describe the rodent contact to them.
