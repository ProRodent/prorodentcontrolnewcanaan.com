What is different about a business.

A business has more doorways than a house. Delivery doors swing open all day, cardboard and trash stack up, and storerooms hold food or packaging. Customers spot a rodent faster than a homeowner would, which is why regular checks make sense.

The aim is a program, not a one-off visit. That means finding why animals come, reducing it and watching for new activity.

How a commercial visit works.

  • Survey the entire property, including back areas and ceilings.
  • Identify the animal and map its routes.
  • Place traps and stations out of reach of staff and customers.
  • Advise on storage, waste and door habits.
  • Seal gaps and entries.
  • Leave written notes after each visit.

Closing the building.

Sealing matters even more in a commercial setting. Entry point sealing closes gaps around pipes, doors and loading areas. Trapping and removal handles live activity. For a first look, an inspection maps the property.

Schedules and records.

We set a visit schedule that suits the business. Notes after each visit record what was found and done, which can help with landlord discussions or health inspections. We do not state what a regulator requires, so check the rules that apply to your business.

Cleanup safety steps from public health agencies

Use this as a first routine whenever droppings, stains or nest material turn up indoors.

  • A filtered vacuum is still the wrong tool until the waste has been soaked.
  • Fresh air comes first. Open the space, wait about half an hour, and only then go in.
  • Germs are invisible, so gloves on, then soap and water when you finish.
  • Wet the droppings and the surface beneath with a household disinfectant, or bleach cut to its label strength, then give it about five minutes before wiping up with paper towels.
  • Put waste in a bag, tie it off and drop it with the household trash. Never lift a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • If flu-like symptoms follow, mention the rodent contact to a physician. Diagnosis is not our work.
