Why gaps matter more than traps.

A rodent that is trapped out of a house is replaced by another unless the opening is closed. Mice need a gap about the width of a pencil. Rats need about half an inch. Houses have far more such gaps than owners expect.

Of everything we do, exclusion is what endures. Done properly, it leaves a house that animals cannot enter, and the signs stop coming back.

Where the survey looks.

  • Roof edges, soffits, ridge vents and chimney flashing.
  • Openings where pipes, wires and gas lines enter walls.
  • Foundation cracks, sill plates and crawl space vents.
  • Garage doors, thresholds and utility doors.
  • Dryer, bath and kitchen vent terminations.
  • Siding joints, trim and the places where porches meet walls.

New Canaan is known for mid-century modern houses, and homes of that kind often have flat or low-slope roofs and wide overhangs. General experience suggests the joins around such roofs, where different materials meet, can hide small openings, so the technician checks them closely. Tell us the roof style and any recent roofing or window work when you call.

The material depends on the size and location of the gap. We avoid spray foam alone, because rodents chew it away within days.

The right order of work.

The sequence is inspect, trap, seal, clean. Sealing waits until trapping shows that the activity has dropped, so no animal is closed inside the structure. A final check confirms that the seals hold and that no fresh sign appears.

For the first stage, see the inspection page. For removal, see trapping and removal. If the animals have already damaged insulation or wiring, rodent damage repair follows.

Keeping the seals working.

Seals last, but houses move. Check vents and door corners each season, and after storms, roofing or tree work. A small gap noticed early costs far less than the problem it invites.

Cleanup safety steps from public health agencies

Rodent waste can carry germs, so technique matters more than speed. The steps follow the CDC cleanup page named at the bottom.

  • Hold the broom. Dry droppings crumble, and sweeping turns them into dust you could inhale.
  • Let outside air flow through a stale crawl space or storeroom for about half an hour first.
  • Surfaces near waste can carry germs you cannot see, which is why gloves and handwashing both matter.
  • Disinfect before you collect. Wet the waste fully and leave it roughly five minutes.
  • Put waste in a bag, tie it off and drop it with the household trash. Never lift a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • Flu-like illness or breathlessness after exposure is a job for a doctor. Mention the cleanup, and leave health judgments to them.

Sealing questions.

Why not use foam or steel wool?

Foam is chewed away and steel wool gets pulled out. Both may hold briefly, but rodents return. Metal flashing and hardware cloth last.

How many openings does a house usually have?

It varies widely. Some houses have a handful, others dozens. The survey counts and lists each one.

Will sealing change how the house looks?

Most work is hidden under trim, behind vents and in attics. Where something shows, the technician explains it first.

Does sealing come before or after trapping?

After catches slow down. Sealing too early can trap animals inside walls.

Can you work on a flat roof or wide overhang?

Yes. Describe the roof style on the call, and the technician plans for access and for the joins between materials.
