How we read a New Canaan property.

New Canaan is a Fairfield County town, incorporated in 1801, that touches Darien, Stamford, Wilton and Norwalk. The Metro-North New Canaan Branch of the New Haven Line serves it. Those facts help you picture the place, but they cannot say what is living in your wall, so each job starts with the building itself.

Some lots here are open and others are thick with plantings, and houses come in many styles. When yours stands among mature trees, a limb touching the roof, shrubs packed against the foundation and firewood stacked on the siding all hand animals a ladder and a hiding place. Ten minutes outdoors with a flashlight shows more than any ZIP code.

Mouse, rat, or neither?

Pencil-width gaps are enough for a house mouse. Norway and roof rats need about half an inch, and they move along fences, cables and branches to reach the roofline. Deer mice like wood edges, sheds and garage corners.

The species decides which trap, sealing material and cleanup method fits. A few words by phone get things started, and the walk-through fills in the rest. Naming the animal is our job, not yours.

Once the nights get cold.

Rodents mostly move after dark, so the first hint usually comes in the evening. Calls are answered overnight, and if the day has room a technician can arrive the same day. Say which room had the sound and about when it started.

As Connecticut nights drop below freezing, mice and rats look for shelter that is warm and dry. A quiet September can turn into a noisy November, and a slow lap around the outside before the first hard freeze is cheap insurance.

  • Check vents, pipe collars and garage door corners once a season.
  • Move kibble, wild bird mix and lawn seed into lidded tubs.
  • Raise firewood and lumber off the ground and away from walls.
  • Cut back branches and vines that brush the roof or lean on siding.

Before anyone comes to the door.

Some quick steps protect your household and keep the evidence readable. Leave droppings alone instead of sweeping or vacuuming them, keep children and pets out of the room and let any traps stay where they are.

  • Take pictures of droppings, tooth marks or damage with a coin beside them.
  • Write down the room and the time of each noise.
  • Shut the door of the room where the sign is heaviest.
  • Pass on loose bait and glue boards, which add new hazards.

What happens during a visit.

The order stays the same whether the job is a single mouse in a pantry or a nest inside a car engine.

  1. Survey.

    On arrival the technician inspects the attic, the basement or crawl space, the garage, the yard and the roof edge, hunting for fresh sign and every opening in use.

  2. Clear.

    Traps and stations are laid where the evidence says animals travel. Each one suits the species at hand and sits where kids and pets cannot reach it.

  3. Close.

    Gaps, vents and chewed holes are closed with sheet metal, mesh and sealant, materials that a rodent's teeth cannot defeat.

  4. Confirm.

    On the return trip we empty traps, test the seals, and handle cleanup or repair for whatever the animals left behind.

Clues that rodents are in your New Canaan house.

Two or more of these in one part of the house suggest current activity rather than a leftover from a past season.

  • Pellets.

    Dark grains the size of rice along a baseboard, in a drawer, on a garage shelf or spread over attic insulation.

  • Teeth marks.

    New gnawing looks pale and rough, whether on lumber, plastic pipe, cable sheathing or food packaging.

  • Sounds after dark.

    Scratching, scurrying or thumping within a wall or above a ceiling once the house goes still.

  • Nesting debris.

    Shredded paper, pulled insulation, scraps of fabric or a mound of dry leaves in a sheltered corner.

  • Grease trails.

    Dark, oily smears on walls, pipes and joists show where an animal runs every night.

  • Smell.

    A sharp, ammonia-like odor in a shut room, or a heavier stench where an animal has died.

Answers for New Canaan households.

How do I tell mice from rats?

Compare size and location. Small pellets inside drawers and cabinets suggest mice, while larger ones along a roofline or across an attic suggest rats. A burrow at a foundation or shed leans toward rats.

Where do rodents get into a house?

Mice squeeze through pencil-width gaps, and rats need roughly double. Frequent entries are openings around pipes and wires, vents, garage door corners, screens at the crawl space and the roof edge.

Will traps alone solve it?

Traps remove animals that are already inside, and sealing stops new ones. Most jobs need both, in that order, and a second visit to confirm that the work held.

Is the work safe around children and pets?

Mention who lives in the home before we start. Traps and stations are set beyond reach, and you are told what was placed and where.

How fast can someone come?

Calls are answered day and night, and a visit may happen the same day if the schedule has room. Describe what you found and ask what is open.

What happens after the first visit?

Traps are emptied and seals tested, and cleanup is discussed. Chewed wiring, ducts or insulation are scoped as a separate repair.
