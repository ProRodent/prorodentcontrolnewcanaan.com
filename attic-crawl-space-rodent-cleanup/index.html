What rodents leave in attics and crawl spaces.

Mice and rats nest in insulation, tear it into runways and use corners as toilets. Over time droppings and urine soak into material and framing. Heat in an attic can intensify the smell on warm days.

The CDC warns that dry rodent waste can release germs into the air when disturbed. That is why sweeping and vacuuming are the wrong tools, and why cleanup uses a damp method and protective gear.

Spaces in New Canaan houses.

Houses differ widely. Some have full attics, some have shallow roof cavities, and a low modern design may have very little attic at all. Crawl spaces and basements vary too. Tell us what your house has, because access and headroom decide the method and the time.

How a cleanup proceeds.

  • Ventilate the space and wait before entering.
  • Wear gloves, eye protection and a respirator suited to the job.
  • Wet nests and droppings with disinfectant first, and lift nothing until they are soaked.
  • Bag waste and soiled insulation in sealed plastic.
  • Treat surfaces and framing, then let the space dry.
  • Replace insulation if it was removed.

This sequence follows public CDC cleanup guidance. We are a pest service and not a health provider, so questions about exposure belong with a doctor.

Safety while you wait.

Keep out of a fouled attic or crawl space until the visit, because disturbed insulation fills the air with dust. If you must enter, ventilate first, put on gloves and a good mask, and leave the vacuum behind. The checklist below summarizes the CDC steps.

Safe order for droppings: what to do and when

Thinking about tidying up first? Go in this sequence, and pause if any step feels uncertain.

  • Leave dry pellets and nest bits alone until they are wet, and do not blow, brush or vacuum them.
  • Treat thirty minutes of airing as part of the job, not an extra.
  • Disposable gloves and reusable rubber ones both work. Wash the gloves, then yourself.
  • Sequence matters. Wet it, wait around five minutes, wipe. That keeps dust down while the product works.
  • Tie the bag shut and keep your skin off the animal.
  • Fever, aches or breathing difficulty after contact with waste deserve a physician's attention. We make no claims about anyone's health.

Cleanup questions.

Can I clean it myself?

Light cleanup is possible if you follow the CDC steps: ventilate, wear gloves, soak waste and bag it. Large areas of droppings and soiled insulation are better left to trained crews.

Do I have to replace insulation?

If it is soaked, matted or heavily soiled, it should come out. Light contamination may be treated. The technician decides on site.

Why wait until after sealing?

Cleaning an attic that animals still use means they will soil it again. Close the gaps first.

Will the smell go away?

Usually yes once waste and soiled material are removed and the area is treated. A dead animal may need separate attention.

Is cleanup a separate price?

Yes. It is quoted apart from trapping and sealing, so each cost shows.
