Why lower levels attract rodents.

A garage offers what animals look for: shelter from weather, a clutter of hiding spots and sometimes a snack. A second refrigerator, a bag of birdseed or a bin of pet food is enough. Once inside, mice use stored cartons for nests and chew insulation on wires.

Basements add warmth and a quiet corner or two. Foundation cracks, old pipe sleeves and window wells give animals a way in from the soil. Few people walk through either room, so a problem can run for weeks unnoticed.

What the technician checks.

  • The overhead door seal, corners and the threshold.
  • The seam where the garage wall meets the slab.
  • Stored boxes, holiday bins, fabrics and paper goods.
  • Pet food, birdseed and grass seed containers.
  • Utility entries for pipes, wires and the dryer vent.
  • Basement foundation cracks, window wells and sump areas.

A quick door test: switch the lights off, stand inside, and scan the bottom edge for daylight. Light shows a gap, and a gap that wide is enough for a mouse.

Trap, then seal, then tidy.

Traps go along the walls where evidence leads, not in open floor. Snap traps and enclosed stations fit tight spots behind shelving. Once catches slow, the gaps are sealed with metal flashing, mesh and sealant, and the door seal is replaced if worn.

Cars, cords and cleanup.

A garage that has rodents can pass them to a car. Look under the hood and at the cabin vents, and read the vehicle page for what to check. Chewed cords and hoses should be replaced, which falls under damage repair.

Droppings piled on shelves or in corners should wait for the damp method in the safety list below. For large amounts, read how attic and crawl space cleanup handles contaminated areas.

Keeping lower levels clear.

Pick one weekend each season to move boxes away from walls, test the door seal and glance at vents. If you see fresh droppings or hear scratching, call. Early action costs less than waiting for a nest.

A brief list based on CDC advice: what to do first

Use this as a first routine whenever droppings, stains or nest material turn up indoors.

  • Disturbing dry droppings is what causes trouble, so keep brooms and shop vacuums out of the picture.
  • Open every door, vent and window you can reach and stay out for at least thirty minutes.
  • Germs are invisible, so gloves on, then soap and water when you finish.
  • Sequence matters. Wet it, wait around five minutes, wipe. That keeps dust down while the product works.
  • Put waste in a bag, tie it off and drop it with the household trash. Never lift a dead rodent with bare hands.
  • Fever, aches or breathing difficulty after contact with waste deserve a physician's attention. We make no claims about anyone's health.

Garage and basement questions.

Why do rodents like garages?

They offer shelter, clutter and often stored food such as pet kibble or birdseed. A worn door seal gives them an easy way in.

Do basements need sealing too?

Yes. Foundation cracks, utility entries and window wells can all admit animals, especially when the weather turns cold.

Should I throw away chewed boxes?

Bag them carefully without shaking them out, and let the technician look first. Droppings inside should be handled with the damp method.

Can a car be affected?

Yes. A car parked in an infested garage may get nests under the hood. See the vehicle page for what to check.

Can this be done at night?

Calls are picked up at all hours, and the next open slot goes to the next job.
