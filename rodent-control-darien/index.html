Across the seasons.

Many people first hear scratching in autumn. Cooler nights send mice and rats hunting for shelter, and a gap overlooked in warm months turns into the easy entrance. Early fall suits a check of the vents, the garage corners and the screens at a crawl space.

Trees, roof edges and what climbs them.

Rats and some mice use branches, cables and shrubs as stairways to a roof. A limb that rests on the roof edge or on siding is an easy bridge, and cutting it back by several feet is often the cheapest step available.

Climbing animals also use vines and thick plantings against a wall. Pull planting back from the foundation, and look at vents and soffits above the point where they climb. The exclusion page lists the materials we use to close those openings.

While you wait: what to do and what to avoid

Rodent waste can carry germs, so technique matters more than speed. The steps follow the CDC cleanup page named at the bottom.

  • Disturbing dry droppings is what causes trouble, so keep brooms and shop vacuums out of the picture.
  • A garage, shed or attic closed up for a while needs air before you work in it, so open it and wait around half an hour.
  • Disposable gloves and reusable rubber ones both work. Wash the gloves, then yourself.
  • Sequence matters. Wet it, wait around five minutes, wipe. That keeps dust down while the product works.
  • Place used towels and any carcass in a plastic bag, close it tightly, and keep skin from touching the animal.
  • Muscle aches, fever or breathlessness after exposure are medical questions and not pest questions.
