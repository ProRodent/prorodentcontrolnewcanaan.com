What residents usually notice.

Small signs are easy to brush off, so let this list prompt you to describe whatever you noticed.

  • Heavy thumping overhead around dusk.
  • Shredded nest material in a vent, engine bay or storage tub.
  • Greasy smears along walls and pipes.
  • A dog or cat fixed on one corner.
  • Droppings that reappear after cleaning.

Pets, feeders and food storage.

Dry pet food left out overnight is a common draw, and so is seed under a feeder. Pick bowls up at night, store kibble in sealed bins and sweep spilled seed daily. These small habits can cut activity before any trap is set.

Safe order for droppings: what to do and when

The points below restate public CDC advice in plain words, and they hold even if you never hire anyone.

  • Soak first and handle later. Dry waste is never swept up.
  • Prop open what you can and let a closed-up space breathe for roughly half an hour.
  • Surfaces near waste can carry germs you cannot see, which is why gloves and handwashing both matter.
  • Saturate the pellets with disinfectant, let them sit five minutes, then take them up with towels.
  • Everything you wiped up goes in a sealed bag before the bin, and a dead animal needs gloves.
  • Muscle aches, fever or breathlessness after exposure are medical questions and not pest questions.
