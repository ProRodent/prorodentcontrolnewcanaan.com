Why start with a look.

A rodent problem rarely announces its size. A single noise in the wall might be one mouse or a nest. Without a look, any price is a guess and any treatment is a gamble. The inspection replaces both with facts.

It also protects you from paying for work you do not need. Some calls turn out to be squirrels, bats or loose flashing flapping in the wind. Knowing that early saves money and time.

What the technician examines.

  • The roof edge, soffits, fascia and chimney flashing.
  • Vents, pipe collars and every wall penetration.
  • The attic, including insulation, framing and ductwork.
  • The basement or crawl space, its door and vents.
  • The garage, its door seal and stored goods.
  • The yard, fence lines, woodpiles, sheds and feeders.

If your house is a modern design with wide overhangs and large glass areas, the technician pays extra attention to roof edges and to joins between different materials, because experience suggests such places can hide small gaps. Tell us the style when you call so we can plan the order of the walk-through.

Reading the evidence.

Fresh droppings are dark and soft, old ones are gray and crumbly. Gnaw marks look pale when new. Rub marks show a route in constant use, and nests show how long animals have been settled. Together these clues say how active the problem is and how fast to move.

How the estimate is built.

A plain-language plan follows the walk-through. Removal leads, sealing comes next, and cleanup or repair trails behind. Every item carries its own price, so you may approve one step or all.

If trapping is needed, the plan says how many visits to expect. If the problem is mostly sealing, you hear that too. For the work itself, see trapping and removal and entry point sealing.

While you wait: what to do and what to avoid

Give the list one read before touching anything. A minute now protects your lungs.

  • Leave dry pellets and nest bits alone until they are wet, and do not blow, brush or vacuum them.
  • Prop open what you can and let a closed-up space breathe for roughly half an hour.
  • Germs are invisible, so gloves on, then soap and water when you finish.
  • Dampen every stain and pellet with disinfectant, then allow about five minutes before lifting them away.
  • Tie the bag shut and keep your skin off the animal.
  • A doctor alone can say how serious an exposure was. Visit one if fever, aches or labored breathing set in.

What to do until we arrive.

  • Hold off starting a car that shows nest material under the hood.
  • For now, store pet food, lawn seed and birdseed inside the house.
  • Bag any chewed object and keep it for the technician.
  • Locate the main electrical panel in case wiring has been gnawed.

Inspection questions.

Must I be there for the visit?

Somebody has to open the attic hatch, the crawl space and the garage. If that person is not you, give us a name when you call.

How long does a walk-through take?

Most are done within an hour or two, depending on the size of the house and how many outbuildings there are.

What if nothing is found?

Then you are told so, and the likely sources of the noise or smell are explained. A clean result is a useful answer too.

Is the price fixed after the visit?

The quote covers the scope described at the visit. If the work reveals something new, you hear about it before any added cost.

Can an inspection happen at night?

Phones are answered at all hours, and visits go to the first opening in the schedule.
